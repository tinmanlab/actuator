02 / FOC CONTROLS CURRENT, NOT POSITION DIRECTLY

Command, measurement and actual current are different signals.

A 4 A q-axis command is applied at 10 ms with both shafts locked. The PI controller changes voltage; resistance and inductance determine how current evolves. The ADC sees a filtered, quantized and delayed observation. The graph keeps each signal at its own timestamp, not a visually convenient shared timestamp.

Use the FOC coordinate calculator before interpreting q-axis current. The amplitude-invariant transform gives:

Pabc = 1.5(vd id + vq iq) Te = 1.5 p[ψm iq + (Ld − Lq) id iq] Pcu = R(ia² + ib² + ic²) = 1.5 R(id² + iq²)

For a balanced rotating sinusoid with id = 0, phase RMS is |iq|/√2. This does not mean each phase at locked rotor has that time RMS. DC-bus current is different again. Here R is per-phase, and flux is per-phase PM linkage; a line-to-line datasheet value cannot be pasted in without checking its convention. [2] [7]

03 / DUTY → SIX GATES → PHASE VOLTAGE

SVPWM is not the voltage waveform itself.

FOC supplies vd/vq. Inverse transforms produce three voltage references. This implementation adds the same common-mode term to all three, then normalizes by the DC bus. Equal offsets cancel in line-to-line voltage; they improve linear bus utilization. [3]

vcommon = −(max(va*, vb*, vc*) + min(va*, vb*, vc*))/2 dphase = 0.5 + (vphase* + vcommon)/Vdc |vdq| ≤ Vdc/√3 × (1 − 2m), m = 0.02 here

At 48 V, the configured vector limit is about 26.60 V, below the ideal 27.71 V because of the 2% duty margin. This is a voltage-vector bound, not phase RMS. Anti-windup tracks the final vector limit; it cannot guarantee stability under arbitrary delay. Controller limits · [4].

Look inside four actual PWM periods.

Choose a phase and a signal. Gates are boolean switch commands, not measured gate-source voltage. High and low gates are both off during dead time. Depending on current direction, a body diode can still conduct. Winding current changes continuously while terminal voltage switches. [5]

Never infer PWM ripple from the live lab’s 1 kHz plot: it displays an averaged inverter. This model includes Rds(on) drop and a fixed diode drop, but not MOSFET rise/fall waveforms, gate charge, switching-energy loss, reverse recovery, ringing or shoot-through. The illustrated PCB is not a validated gate-driver circuit. [6]

04 / GATES OFF ≠ CURRENT INSTANTLY ZERO

Stored energy still needs a path.

The next experiment injects a driver fault at 80 ms. The latch inhibits gates; the diode topology solver lets current decay rather than setting it to zero. At sufficient back-EMF, gate-off can also rectify energy into the bus. This locked-rotor example only demonstrates current extinction.

Independent FastTrip observes phase current outside the delayed ADC path. Comparator, blanking, BREAK, gate-off and supervisor acknowledgement have separate timestamps. Blanking can hide a real pulse; integration resolution still limits crossing-time evidence. STM32 automatic-output-enable options are configurable, so our explicit no-auto-rearm rule is a design choice, not a universal MCU guarantee. [10] [11]

05 / SECONDS OF MOTION, MINUTES OF HEATING

A cold motor and a warm motor are not the same plant.

Winding copper loss raises winding temperature; heat flows to the case and ambient. Resistance rises with temperature, increasing the voltage needed to hold current. A separate single RC state represents aggregate FET heating. [7]

R(Tw) = R25[1 + α(Tw − 25°C)], α = 0.00393/K Cw dTw/dt = Pcu − (Tw − Tc)/Rwc Cc dTc/dt = (Tw − Tc)/Rwc − (Tc − Ta)/Rca Cf dTf/dt = Pbridge − (Tf − Ta)/Rfa

Why distinguish this from an operating drive? A drive should reduce current before reaching its trip temperature. The existing thermal bench starts the winding at 105°C to test derating quickly; it is not evidence that a cold motor heats to 105°C in milliseconds.

Only copper loss heats the winding model. Mechanical friction is removed as dissipated power, not routed into those thermal nodes. Iron/eddy losses, magnet-temperature effects, winding hotspots and PCB cooling geometry are omitted. Real continuous ratings require those effects and measured thermal parameters; this graph cannot establish a motor’s safe continuous torque. [8] [9]

06 / TORQUE IS NOT POWER; POWER IS NOT EFFICIENCY

Account for the watts before quoting a percentage.

The plant has separate motor/output inertias, a 6:1 gearbox, backlash and elastic coupling. Friction is smooth Coulomb plus viscous drag: Tf = bω + Tcoulomb tanh(ω/ε). This avoids a discontinuous sign but does not implement true static friction, stick-slip or Stribeck behavior. [12]

The example below commands 3 rad/s and adds a 1 N·m output load at 300 ms. The higher-friction case triples only output viscous drag from 0.02 to 0.06 N·m·s/rad; it is not a different motor product.

Pdc = Vdc Idc Pac = va ia + vb ib + vc ic Pdc − Pac = Pbridge (same evaluation state) Pac ≈ Pcu + Pfriction + Pgear + Pload + dEstored/dt ηmotoring = mean(Pload) / mean(Pdc), only in a stated quasi-steady motoring window

Boundary: inverter DC terminals → external load. Supply ESR, brake resistor and logic/driver auxiliary consumption are outside this ratio. The omitted iron and switching losses make it unsuitable as a commercial efficiency estimate. We report the remaining numerical energy residual instead of assigning it to an invented loss channel. At standstill, torque may be nonzero while Pload = Tload ωoutput = 0.

Regeneration reverses the power flow.

A velocity command drops from 5 to 0 rad/s at 300 ms. Negative inverter DC power means energy is returning to the link capacitor. The modeled source cannot sink current; a hysteretic brake resistor switches on at 53 V and off at 51 V. Those are example settings, not a protection design for a real board.
